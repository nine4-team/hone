### App-model reframe (product decision, not yet built)

The "hit list" is your **active skills** (the moves you're hunting) — NOT the people you've
landed moves on. The people leaderboard keeps its data but becomes a secondary **Partners**
view. Reason: a hit list is a list of *targets you're hunting*, which matches the philosophy
("one skill at a time") and the core loop; "people you've already hit" is a backward-looking
trophy, the wrong thing to name the product after. App-side changes (screens, tabs, icons,
store hooks, types) are being scoped separately via an agent change-list prompt.

Working doc for the landing-page hero. Cold visitors land here with **no context**, so the
hero has to orient (what is this / who's it for) *and* promise a benefit in the first two seconds.

## Principles

- **Promise the outcome, not the mechanism.** Visitors came to get better, faster. "Hits,"
  "skills," and "the list" are *how* it works — they belong in the sub and the body, not the H1.
- **Orient before you hook.** The eyebrow should establish category (it's an app, it's for
  jiu-jitsu) so the headline is free to do persuasion.
- **Don't make it all about beating other people.** Outpacing peers can be a flavor, not the
  whole thesis.

## Page alignment (why the hero changed)

The philosophy section opens on **love** — beat 01: "you train because you love developing *your
game*" — and doesn't reach the plateau/quality problem until beat 04. A problem-led hero (doubt or
frustration) reverses that tone and leaves the doubt it plants unanswered until four beats down.

**Decision:** open the hero on the same desire the philosophy is built on — getting better at the
game you love — and plant the phrase **"your game,"** which beats 01–02 then pay off directly. The
plateau/quality argument stays where it lands hardest: the turn at beat 04, as *proof* the promise
is real, not as the first thing a stranger feels.

## Layer roles (locked)

Three layers, each with one job. Don't let them overlap.

- **Eyebrow — who it's for.** Just call out the audience. No qualifiers, no "app" language.
  - **Jiu-jitsu athletes and hobbyists** ← pick. Names both ends of the audience: a competitor
    and a casual hobbyist both see themselves. "For grapplers" is narrower and reads hardcore-only.
- **H1 — the hook.** Pure persuasion. No orientation burden (the other two layers carry it).
- **Sub — what it is + how it works.** This is where "the training app that…" lives.

> Parked: clever/label-style eyebrows ("the jiu-jitsu training app", "made deliberate",
> "smarter training"). Empty qualifiers; the eyebrow just names the audience.

## Rejected / parked

- **"Improve faster by building your Hit List"** (original) — mechanism-as-benefit, vague.
- **"A move isn't yours until you've hit it live"** — possession metaphor is abstract; faint
  scold; too focused on a single move rather than a benefit.
- **"Pick a skill. Hunt the hit. Grow your game."** — strong verb sequence but scoped to single
  moves, not the broader benefit.
- **Outpace-your-peers angle** ("Improve faster than the people you started with") — good flavor,
  too narrow as the whole thesis.
