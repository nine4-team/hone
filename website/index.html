The Method

Why most people stall at jiu jitsu — and how you won't.

01 — The goal

The belt isn't the point.

You're not training for a stripe. You're training to get better — and the only question that matters is whether you actually are.

There's an honest answer: can you land a move you couldn't land before, live, on someone trying to stop you? That's a hit.

More hits, on tougher people, and you're getting better. If not, you're not.

02 — The problem

But most people never get there.

They show up to class, drill whatever the instructor shows, roll without a focus, and forget most of it by next week.

Training with no intention is just random reps — and random reps make you better slowly, by accident, if at all.

Don't leave your progress to chance. Train with intention.

03 — The fix

The solution is the Skill Equation.

Training improvement comes down to how much you train and how well you train.

The equation
Improvement = training volume × training quality

Improve either factor and you get better faster. It comes down to how you structure your training, and how you spend each session on the mat.

Training Habits
  • Commit to a few skills. Pick a small set worth improving and stay on them for weeks — not whatever the class happens to cover.
  • Train them the right way. How you practice matters more than how much — quality reps, not mindless ones.
  • Get the volume. Quantity is the other half of the equation: the more live attempts you get, the faster a skill lands.
  • Track your hits. Count what lands live — your feedback on what's working and what to fix next.
Training Tools
  • Study. Get the blueprint from instructionals and footage, then break down your own rolls to see where you stand and what's missing.
  • Dialogue drilling. Interactive problem-solving with a reacting partner — where the mechanics actually click and you learn why a move works.
  • Constraint games. Live-resistance games scoped to a single exchange, so you bank tons of real reps right where it counts.
  • Live rolling. Full resistance. Where you prove a skill works — and where the hits happen.
